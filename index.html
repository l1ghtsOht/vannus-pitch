Insight

01

What is it?

Vannus is the jurisdiction record for AI vendors. For each AI tool a company uses, we establish four facts and cite every one of them:

1. The contracting entity — the legal person you actually sign with, which is frequently not the brand on the website. 2. That entity's country of incorporation. 3. The governing law the agreement names. 4. Therefore: which government can compel disclosure of the data that vendor holds.

Each determination quotes the vendor's own published terms, with a link to the source and the date it was checked. Where a vendor doesn't publish enough to establish a fact, the record says "not disclosed" instead of inferring it.

The buyer is not shopping. They have been asked a question they cannot answer — by a customer's Data Processing Agreement, a security questionnaire, a Transfer Impact Assessment, an auditor, or since 2025 a DORA Register of Information. They search, they get a confident answer, and they write it into a document that carries legal weight.

Who's the customer, and what do I know that others don't?

The customer is whoever owns the answer when the question arrives: a privacy or security lead, a compliance manager, a DPO, or at a smaller company the founder who inherited it. They are not buying software. They are buying the ability to answer, in writing, with a source.

Hosting location is not legal control — and the vendor's own lawyer said so under oath

Anton Carniaux, Directeur des affaires publiques et juridiques at Microsoft France, appeared before the French Senate's commission of inquiry on Tuesday 10 June 2025. He was sworn — the president had him raise his right hand. Rapporteur Dany Wattebled asked whether he could guarantee that data of French citizens entrusted to Microsoft via UGAP would never be transmitted following a US government injunction without the explicit agreement of French authorities.

“Non, je ne peux pas le garantir, mais, encore une fois, cela ne s'est encore jamais produit.”

“No, I cannot guarantee it — but, once again, it has never yet happened.”

I quote the second half deliberately, because it is the part that matters. The defence offered by the vendor's own counsel, under oath, is not that the data is protected. It is that nobody has asked yet. That is not a legal protection. It is a waiting period.

Two cases that show why a search doesn't solve this

OpenAI splits by region twice, and the two splits are not the same split. Its business terms name OpenAI OpCo, LLC for customers outside the EEA or Switzerland and OpenAI Ireland Ltd for customers inside them. The governing-law clause names the laws of Ireland for customers in the EEA, Switzerland or the UK — and California for everyone else.

The UK is in the second list and not the first. A UK company writing “our AI subprocessor is OpenAI Ireland, governed by Irish law” into a DPA is right about the law and wrong about the entity — and the entity is what determines whether a US government demand reaches the data.

Microsoft 365 Copilot: a web search and a general AI assistant both answer confidently, and both answer from the wrong document. Ask either and you get “you're contracting with Microsoft Corporation, One Microsoft Way, Redmond.” That sentence is from the consumer Microsoft Services Agreement. It does not govern the business product.

Vannus publishes no contracting entity for Microsoft 365 Copilot — no entity, no country, no governing law, on the page or in the API. The most recognisable vendor in the catalog, the one a reader is most likely to check first, and the field is empty because it could not be established from public documents. That refusal is the product. In a filing that goes to a regulator, a confident wrong answer is worse than a blank — and a general-purpose model will always produce the confident answer, because that is what it is for.

What I do not yet know: I have not interviewed these buyers. See question 7. That is the honest gap in this application and I am not going to paper over it.

Two barriers hold the space open

First, the work is unglamorous and manual. One record means finding which of a vendor's several agreements applies, locating the contracting clause, quoting it, linking the source, dating it — and often concluding you cannot establish it. It does not demo well and it cannot be scraped reliably, because the answer is frequently conditional on the buyer's region and purchase channel.

Second, everyone else's incentive is to answer. A platform that returns “not disclosed” for a marquee vendor looks broken. For us it is the proof of method. That asymmetry is defensible precisely because it is commercially uncomfortable.

Honest counter: the inputs are public. OpenAI publishes its own entity split; the EU–US Data Privacy Framework list names certified US entities; Open Terms Archive versions vendor terms for free. A large incumbent could add a contracting-entity field. What they would also have to add is the willingness to publish blanks, and the corpus of already-checked determinations. The first is cultural; the second is time.

Method note The absence of an incumbent is a negative search result. It is not proof that none exists.

Why now, and how big?

Three forcing functions, all dated, none of them mine

DORA became applicable 17 January 2025. Article 28(3) requires every EU financial entity to maintain a Register of Information covering all ICT third-party contracts and to produce it to the competent authority on request. The machine-readable templates — including the provider's country of headquarters (mandatory for every provider), and for contracts supporting a critical or important function the country of provision, of data at rest, and of processing — are set by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2956 under Art. 28(9). The annual cycle and the 31 December reference date come from the ESAs' Joint Decision of November 2024. I separate these because Art. 28(3) alone does not contain the country fields, and the person I am selling to is exactly the person with it open.

The ESAs ran a voluntary dry run in 2024. 1,039 financial entities across 27 Member States submitted registers. 92 were rejected outright on integration checks. Of the 947 that were analysed, 6.5% passed all 116 data-quality checks — about sixty registers. Half the remainder failed fewer than five checks, and the ESAs said they were confident the data would be of sufficient quality. I give the exact denominator because 6.5% is of 947, not of the 1,039 in the sentence before it — and because the softer reading is the true one: these institutions are not incapable. The fields are simply hard to source.

EDPB Recommendations 01/2020 (v2.0, adopted 18 June 2021) require the exporter to assess the law and practice of the importer's own country — specifically any law compelling that importer to disclose personal data to public authorities — and to document it (paras 30–35, 48). The EDPB never uses the word “extraterritorial.” It does not have to: once the test is the law binding the entity, hosting location has already stopped being the answer. The assessment is mandatory. There is no standard evidence source for it.

And the supply side is measured only by an interested party — I will label it that way rather than let you find out. DataGrail, a privacy software vendor, reviewed 2,400 business systems for its 2026 report and states that 63.6% of AI vendors do not disclose subprocessors in their legal documentation. DataGrail publishes no methodology, so I use it as a direction, not as evidence. IAPP covered it in July 2026 but did not measure it independently. It is the only public measurement of this gap I have found.

08b

How does revenue compound?

Three levers, and only the first is structural rather than hopeful.

1. The corpus compounds and never depreciates to zero

Every determination is researched once and sold indefinitely. At 94 records the catalog answers a fraction of any real stack; at 374 it answers most of one. Each added record raises the value of every report that would otherwise have contained a blank — the classic case where coverage is the product.

2. The re-issue is imposed by a regulator, not invented by me

DORA's register is filed annually against a 31 December reference date. A record dated last year does not evidence this year's filing. That makes the natural repeat annual and externally scheduled — the difference between this and the subscription product I killed earlier this year, where the recurrence was my idea and nobody wanted it.

I have pre-committed, in writing, not to build the recurring product until ten $299 reports have been delivered. Zero delivered. So I have not built it, and I will not.

3. Tier movement

The record answers “where do we stand.” The review answers “is the answer we are about to file right.” The audit answers “what do we do about it.” They are sequential, not alternatives. I have no attach-rate evidence, so I am not modelling it.
